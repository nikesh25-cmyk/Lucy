# Built-in research agents produce proposed decision briefs

The worker controls now appear on open mission tasks, including linked follow-ups, and on activated agenda tasks. Choose the existing evidence register/synthesis or a proposed decision brief. A decision brief requires a configured local model, a human-specified proposed recipient/requested decision and one to five approved public HTTPS sources. The mission supplies the intended beneficiary and acceptance criteria. No institution’s agreement is inferred from these inputs.

In decision-brief mode, the model proposes cited findings with qualifications, two decision options, a recommendation rationale, proposed roles and milestones, and unresolved evidence. Normal shared brief validation checks citations, bounds and structure. Recipient, requested decision and beneficiaries come from the human/mission context, replacing any model attempt to change them. Responsible roles must remain explicitly proposed. Model-created cost scenarios are refused; gather and independently review actual cost evidence before entering a cost register. Statistical model prose may not contain digits when statistical sources are present; exact source values remain in retained retrieval notes.

Both local adapters accept a bounded output budget of 128–1,800 tokens. Defaults remain 256 for Ollama and 700 for llama.cpp. Decision briefs request 1,800; the shared one-active/eight-waiting queue, cancellation, pre-dispatch authority checks, worker time/call limits and local-only inference remain. The larger output permits a structured response, not additional agent permissions. Invalid/truncated model responses or unsupported citations fail the brief run and release its claim; no accepted result or fabricated fallback brief is created. Evidence-register mode keeps its existing fallback behavior for validated statistics.

The submitted structured brief retains up to five source retrieval notes: source index, digest, retrieval time, declared publication note, evidence kind and bounded text summary. Web excerpts stay within the reader’s short-quote limit; reviewed PDF summaries are paraphrases and statistical summaries preserve normalized values. Notes may be shortened to 1,800 characters each and are not full source archives. The display, plain-text copy, account history and downloaded briefing show the notes. Public access, retained hashes and model citations do not establish factual support, current applicability or reuse permission.

Retained notes are optional additions to the shared brief schema, with existing briefs remaining readable. A human correction form does not silently copy the prior retrieval notes onto replacement source URLs; prior submission versions preserve their original notes. Uploaded/entered notes are contributor declarations rather than authenticated retrieval receipts. The worker itself derives its notes from the actual read results. The existing 16,000-character serialized brief limit applies to the full brief and notes.

All agent submissions remain pending independent human review. The workflow does not contact recipients, approve shipments, authorize budgets or count a recommendation as adopted. The worker currently requires evidence collection and synthesis in one bounded run; it is not an unattended research swarm.

## Verification

The 162-test suite covers bounds, scoped credentials, one managed job, invalid-output claim release and separation of statistical observations from generated prose. An isolated real Qwen3-4B browser run produced one pending structured brief and one completed job with a valid audit chain. That run demonstrates workflow operation, not factual entailment or official acceptance. Its unsupported qualitative cost labels were identified during review; the final adapter always replaces generated option cost bases with an explicit unknown-cost statement. The recipient was fictional and no external contact occurred.
