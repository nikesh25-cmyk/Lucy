# The Harr Almanac

*A year-book of the village of Harr: where it stands, who lives there, and how its days go.*

## How Harr began

Harr is a research village in a green valley. It was started on a simple idea, written down in the Harr Charter: people can learn together, build together and share what they have. Progress here is counted in knowledge found, things built, food shared and people welcomed. Nobody is asked to hurry past a neighbour, or to hold anything back.

The first public work was the Plaza well, which took three stages and a small crew. Everything else grew from it. Water on the square gave people a reason to meet, and that led to a market hall and to homes for the neighbours who kept arriving. Today the village has a catalogue of thirty-six public works, from the well to the valley tram halt, which together make up one hundred and twenty-five stages. A stage is built only while two or more people stand at its site, so nothing in Harr is ever raised by one pair of hands alone. Each stage becomes ready ten minutes after the one before it, and the village simply waits for a crew.

## The six principles

The Charter names six things Harr cares about, and the almanac keeps them in the order the village does.

**Scholarship.** We write down what we learn so the next person starts further on.

**Research.** Every find and every finished building teaches the village something, and insight turns into new knowledge for everyone.

**Cooperation.** Big works need many hands.

**Growth.** Harr is never finished. New homes, workshops and farms keep rising.

**Discovery.** The valley is full of things worth finding, and every find adds a page for all.

**Abundance.** There is enough. The pantry is open to everyone and the farms restock it.

## Places to know

**The plaza** is the middle of Harr. The well stands here, with the market hall beside it, and the festival and the market hour both gather here. If you are ever lost, walk to the plaza and ask anyone.

**The Plaza well and the hidden spring.** The well is fed by an underground seam from a cold, clear spring a short walk north-east of the plaza. The spring stays at seven degrees all year, which is why the well is never really empty.

**The market hall** is a roofed hall where makers and farmers share what they have grown and made.

**The cottages.** Nine cottages stand in rows and rings around the village. The first three are homes built by the whole village, the next three are split-level eco-cottages with solar skylights, and the last three are sun-warmed stepped domes ringed with flowering planters. The travellers' guesthouse welcomes newcomers with clean beds, hot tea and quiet reading rooms.

**The Harr Library** is where every discovery is written down. The annex gave it more shelves, and the open archive keeps every finding, plan and story for anyone to read, and for other villages to learn from. The research tower gathers the village's findings into one bright view, and the schoolhouse teaches reading, history and botany to young and old together.

**The star observatory** is a raised stone platform where the scholars chart the sky. Until it was ready, they used the star meadow, the darkest corner of Harr.

**The fields and the pantry.** The vertical farm, the glass garden and the orchard feed the village. The pantry offers plain food and nothing else: apples, carrots, cheese and lettuce, a few of each to a person, free for everyone. The solar arrays and the quiet wind array keep the lamps lit.

**The gathering places.** The field pavilion is a shaded, open place for rest and shared meals. The hillside amphitheatre faces the valley, and the festival lawn is framed by lamp arches for communal feasts and round dances. The light fountain is where neighbours sit at the end of the day, and the seminar pavilion is an open ring of columns for talking ideas through.

**The ways between.** The lantern path lights the way to the fields, the sky walk lifts neighbours above the square, and the canal stone bridge joins the plaza to the western workshops. The sky transit hub and the valley tram halt wait for the quiet trains of the future.

## Ten pages of the valley

The library catalogue has ten discovery places, each a short walk from the plaza. Finding one adds a page for everyone, and every one of them can be found by anyone.

- **The wildflower meadow** has seven kinds of flower in bands by soil, and the bees visit the yellow band first.
- **The old boundary stone** is older than the village, and its lichen grows only on the north face.
- **The hidden spring** feeds the well.
- **The high view** shows the valley as a map and the places where cottages could stand.
- **The bee hollow** is a hollow oak that hums with a calm colony whose honey feeds the village.
- **The lichen wall** draws a slow map on stone, and the colours show the air is clean.
- **The birdsong grove** sings eleven voices at dawn, and the scholars add one new voice a week to their score of the chorus.
- **The clay bank** gives fine red clay, and the makers dig only what they need.
- **The star meadow** is dark enough for the whole sky.
- **The wind ridge** has a steady breeze, and the library holds plans for a small mill.

Each place has four field notes from the scholars, and each season adds a few more.

## The people

Harr has twenty-four residents, and each has a work and a way of speaking.

**Builders** raise the public works with a crew. They are practical, proud and careful, and they will show anyone how to lash a post. **Farmers** grow the food the pantry shares, and they know the weather, the soil and the bees. **Makers** craft tools, furniture, cloth, clay and lamps, and they lay out what they have made at market hour. **Scholars** record discoveries and push the research queue forward, and they love questions. **Couriers** carry news between places, so nobody is left out, and they bring the day's chronicle to whoever asks. **Keepers** host the gatherings, look after the shared spaces and lead the story circle.

Two names are worth learning early. Lyra is the archive scholar who keeps the house by the library and walks the Quiet Survey with newcomers; it is a practice walk and awards nothing. Selene is the guide, who tells you about works, the library, the pantry, today's rhythm, the chronicle, your tasks, the village's purpose and its research. She only reads and tells, and she cannot give or change anything.

Villagers keep to a daily round, from dawn through the day and dusk to night. Builders are at the market hall and the well at dawn, at the cottages and the annex by day, and at the orchard gate and the observatory at dusk. Farmers begin at the vertical farm and the solar array, and end in the glass garden. Keepers begin at the transit hub at dawn, the open archive by day, and the light fountain at dusk.

## The rhythm of the week

Harr keeps three gatherings, on the host computer's clock, and everyone is welcome at all of them.

**Market hour** begins at the top of every hour and lasts twelve minutes. Makers, farmers and couriers lay out what they have grown and made on the plaza, and everyone may look, ask and share.

**Story circle** begins at half past each hour and lasts twelve minutes at the field pavilion. Keepers and scholars read aloud what the village has learned and listen to anyone who wants to add a page.

**The festival** is held every Sunday at three in the afternoon, for an hour on the plaza. The whole village gathers to celebrate what has been built and found.

The seasons dress these gatherings differently: seedlings in clay pots in spring, berries and shade in summer, seed swaps in autumn, and lamp-lit stalls with hot tea in winter.

## How the village learns

The village earns insight from facts that were recorded, never invented: two for each place found, one for each building stage, three for each finished project and one for each gathering held. Insight belongs to everyone and is never spent. As it grows, the research queue is learned in order: thirty technologies from field notes and soil science to clean water works, solar lanterns, composting, vertical farming and the open archive, up to the universal lexicon and the harmonic village commons. Learning something new can make the pantry restock faster or hold more.

The Charter sets seven shared goals: a home for every neighbour, clear water on the square, a roofed market, the valley catalogued in ten places, a hundred portions shared, thirty gatherings held, and ten new things learned. A visitor can add to them in an afternoon.

## What to do on a first day

Walk to the plaza and say hello to five villagers. Ask Selene for an apple. Visit one place from the library and stay a few seconds. Then stand beside a crew at a building site and watch a stage go up. By evening you will have met neighbours, eaten at the village table and added a page to the library.

That is all Harr asks. The rest, as the builders like to say, is a matter of many hands.
