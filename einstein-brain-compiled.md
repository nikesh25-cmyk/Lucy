# Einstein's Brain: Compiled Details

Compiled 2026-10-15 from publicly fetchable sources. Slide images themselves are NOT included (see "Access").

## 1. Custody and preparation
- Einstein died 18 Apr 1955 (Princeton Hospital). Pathologist Thomas Stoltz Harvey removed the brain at autopsy; weight **1,230 g**.
- Fixation: ~50% formalin injected via internal carotid arteries, then suspended whole in 10% formalin. Photographed from many angles before cutting.
- Sectioning (Wikipedia): ~**240 blocks**, ~1 cm³ each, embedded in collodion. (AP-based CBC coverage: "approximately 170 parts" cut into hundreds of sections. Sources disagree.)
- Harvey also removed the eyes and gave them to ophthalmologist Henry Abrams.
- Consent: disputed. Hans Albert Einstein approved afterward, only for research in high-standing journals.
- 1978: journalist Steven Levy found sections in two mason jars (alcohol) in a cider box. Later road trip documented by Michael Paterniti (*Driving Mr. Albert*) and Carolyn Abraham (*Possessing Genius*).

## 2. Where the material is now
- **NMHM** (National Museum of Health and Medicine; Silver Spring MD, with a Chicago satellite "NMHM Chicago"): Harvey's heirs transferred slides, archives and 14 previously unseen whole-brain photographs in **2010**.
- **Mütter Museum, Philadelphia**: 46 slides donated by neuropathologist Lucy Rorke-Adams (CHOP), who got the box in the mid-1970s from the widow of a physician involved in preparing the samples. Displayed since 2013 (announced Nov 2011). Rorke-Adams said the tissue "does not show any of the changes that we associate with age."

## 3. Slide digitization
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Scanner / tech | Leica Biosystems Aperio ePathology (model, resolution, file size not published) |
| Count | "Nearly 350" (AP, Sept 2012, app launch) vs "over 550" (Aperio release, Aug 2013). Likely different scopes/stages |
| Slide size | ~1 in x 3 in |

| Led by / designed | Jacopo Annese (UCSD Brain Observatory) led digitization work on comparable projects; app designed by Steve Landers (NMHM Chicago consultant) |

| Public product | **Einstein Brain Atlas** iPad app ("NMHMC - Harvey"), launched ~25 Sept 2012, $9.99, built by Eolas Technologies (Tyler TX); profits go to both museums. Slides grouped by general brain region |
| Other viewer | "Vscope" virtual microscope at NMHM, shown in the 2013 exhibit "What Can We Learn from a Brain" |
| Limitation | No MRI/3D reference, so slide-to-anatomy location is imprecise. Slides cover only a fraction of the brain |
| People quoted | Michael Doyle (NMHM Chicago founder), Jared Schwartz (Leica CMO), Phillip Epstein (neuroscientist), Jim Paglia (museum board) |

## 4. Research findings
| Year | Authors | Material | Finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1985 | Diamond et al. | 4 sections vs 11 male controls (avg ~64 y) | Higher glia:neuron ratio; significant only in left inferior parietal area |
| 1999 | Witelson, Kigar, Harvey (*Lancet*) | 1955 photographs | Parietal lobes ~15% wider; truncated Sylvian fissure / absent parietal operculum |
| 2001 | Zaidel | 2 hippocampal slices | Left hippocampal neurons larger than right |
| 2013 | Falk, Lepore, Noe (*Brain* 136) | 14 unpublished photographs | Normal size; unusual folding in prefrontal, somatosensory, motor, parietal, temporal, occipital cortex; extra mid-frontal ridge; motor-cortex "knob" possibly linked to musical ability |
| 2013 | Men, Falk et al. (*Brain* 137, e268) | Corpus callosum photos vs 15 elderly + 52 young controls | Thicker callosum |
| 2019 | Rutgers Univ. Press chapter | n/a | Densely packed neurons in prefrontal/parietal regions |

Note: Wikipedia labels the Falk study "2012" (online first); the print volume is 2013. Dates above follow print year. I could not fetch PubMed abstracts (blocked), so details are from the secondary summary and should be checked against the papers.

## 5. Criticism
- n=1 vs small control groups; age mismatch (controls ~12 y younger in Diamond); researchers not blinded; publication bias; post-hoc feature hunting (Terence Hines, "neuromythology"; S.S. Kantha). Causation vs lifelong experience is unresolvable.

## 6. Access
- No public web archive of the scans was found. Museum pages (medicalmuseum.health.mil) block automated fetching with a Web Application Firewall. Images exist in the paid iPad app and the museum's gallery viewer.
- Route to full set: research-access request to NMHM Otis Historical Archives.
